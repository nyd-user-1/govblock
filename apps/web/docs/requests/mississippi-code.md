# A request for the Mississippi Code of 1972 in bulk

**To:** Mississippi Joint Legislative Committee on Compilation, Revision and Publication of Legislation
**From:** Brendan Stanton, GovBlock — brendan@nysgpt.com — https://gov.nysgpt.com
**Date:** 12 September 2026
**Status:** drafted, not sent

## What is asked for

A copy of the Mississippi Code of 1972 in bulk, in whatever machine-readable form the Committee already
holds it — XML, SGML, the typesetting files, a database export, or the files
sent to the publisher. Any format is workable. Nothing needs to be prepared
specially; whatever exists is enough.

What is wanted is the statute alone: the text of each section, its number, and
the headings the Code itself gives the titles, chapters and articles above it.

## What is not asked for

The publisher's editorial layer is not wanted and will not be taken:
annotations, case notes, headnotes, research references, indexes and
cross-reference apparatus prepared by LexisNexis are theirs. If the only copy
that exists carries them, they will be stripped and discarded before anything
is stored, and the record will say so.

## What it is for

GovBlock is an open mirror of the standing law of all fifty states, the
District of Columbia and the United States Code, free to read at
https://gov.nysgpt.com/laws and free to reuse. Forty-eight of the fifty-two are
already loaded, every one from its own government's published source. Mississippi Code of 1972
is one of four that publish only through a vendor's viewer, which cannot be
read by a machine below the level of a title.

The data is published under CC BY 4.0 with the source and its currency named on
every page. If the Committee would prefer particular attribution, a currency statement,
or a notice about the authenticated edition, it will be carried verbatim.

## The alternative, if bulk is not possible

If there is no bulk copy to give, two smaller things would do instead:

1. Permission, in writing, to read the free public-access edition
   systematically at a rate the Committee sets, and the name of a contact at
   LexisNexis who can confirm it.
2. Or a pointer to any existing machine-readable edition — a state archive,
   a legacy FTP site, or the files deposited with a library under the state's
   preservation arrangements.
