# Clips: three ways a clip comes to be — recorded, cut, and generated

Brendan, 2026-09-14. This brief is your scope only. Read
`components/clips/` whole first (store.ts carries the wiring plan), then
`lib/policy/cloudflare-stream.ts` and `app/api/stream/route.ts`, then the
two repositories below on GitHub, README and licence, before a line is
written.

## What /clips is today

Short vertical video on the record: desks (New York, Texas, California,
GovBlock, House Agriculture) with clips under them, a feed and a grid,
likes, comments, follows, and Record, which shoots in the browser. It is a
mock: the recording sits in IndexedDB, the reactions in localStorage, the
published set is five stock clips. Cloudflare Stream is the intended store
(direct creator upload, `listVideos` by reader id). Clips is open to every
reader; nothing there needs an account yet.

## What it becomes

A clip has three origins, and all three live in the same feed under the
same desks, with the same likes and comments:

1. **Recorded.** What exists: a reader on camera, in the browser. Keep it,
   and wire it: `saveClip` becomes the Stream direct upload the store
   describes, `loadMine` reads the reader's own videos back.

2. **Cut.** [zhouxiaoka/autoclip](https://github.com/zhouxiaoka/autoclip):
   a long video goes in, a transcript is made (Whisper), a language model
   scores the segments, and the strong ones come out as captioned short
   clips. The record's own raw material is hearing video, hours of it on
   committee and hearing pages that nobody watches whole. Point autoclip
   at a hearing and the desk for that jurisdiction gets its clips, each
   keyed to the hearing, the bill and the roll call, with the record's own
   links under it. The same pipe takes a reader's upload at its front:
   Upload beside Record, and the upload is cut the same way.

3. **Generated.** [remotion-dev/remotion](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion):
   video as React components, previewed in the browser with `<Player>`,
   rendered on a server. Over rows the site already serves: a bill's
   history as a thirty-second timeline, a roll call as an animated tally,
   a member's votes on one subject across a session, a hearing's
   witnesses and their three sentences. Templates, each fed by an id the
   way a block is. A desk fills in a bill number and gets a finished clip;
   a creator picks a template and their own words go in the slots.

## The order

Remotion first: it is TypeScript over data the site holds, and the first
template is a week. Cut second: it is a service to run (Python, ffmpeg,
Whisper, a language model), on a worker box, not in Next.

## Decisions

- **The store is Cloudflare Stream** for every origin. A clip row in Aurora
  carries its Stream id, its origin (`recorded`, `cut`, `generated`), its
  desk, and what it is keyed to (hearing, bill, roll call, member) as the
  record's ids, never as free text. Write the migration under `sql/`,
  additive, and say so in the report before it runs.
- **Rights.** The record's own video is public domain; a reader's upload is
  not ours. Upload takes a checkbox that says the reader has the right to
  post it, and a clip has a report-and-takedown path from its menu, before
  the first upload is possible. No upload without both.
- **Licences.** autoclip's licence goes in the report with the exact
  terms before it is used. Remotion is free for individuals and companies
  of up to three people and licensed above that; the report names the
  licence the product needs and its price, and the lead carries that to
  Brendan before the render server exists. The `<Player>` preview in the
  browser is where the first template is proven.
- **The cut runs on a worker box**, never on the dev box or the pipeline
  box, under nohup with a log. The first hearing it cuts is one Brendan
  names; the report shows its clips before any desk is filled.
- **The Reporter's voice.** A generated clip's words come from the record
  (the bill's title, the action, the vote) or from the reader; the
  product never speaks in the first person and never names the model.

## Done looks like

Record writes to Stream and reads back. One Remotion template (a roll
call as a tally) previews in the browser on a real roll call and renders
once on a server to a Stream video that appears under a desk. One hearing
is cut by autoclip on the worker box into clips under its desk, each
keyed to the hearing. Upload exists behind the rights checkbox. Every
clip, whatever its origin, plays in the same feed with the same reactions.
